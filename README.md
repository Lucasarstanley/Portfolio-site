# Lucas Stanley portfolio site

Plain static site: vanilla JS and three.js, no build step. The whole site is one plane: home at the origin, the Work column one screen down (continuous scroll), Contact one screen to the right, About one screen to the left. Who the site is about (name, the one job title, company, city, email, LinkedIn, the ask on the Contact screen) lives in `site.js`; the `<title>`, description and og: tags in `index.html` repeat the title and description by hand.

The home screen is a ground plane that fills the window, seen at the isometric angle, with the crowd on it. The mouse is the sun's place in the sky: centre is straight overhead, the screen edge is `sun.edgeElevation` degrees above the horizon on that side, and shadows always fall away from the mouse. The mouse also sets the mood (Work top-left, Contact top-right, cold at the bottom), which drives colour, intensity, bloom and crowd behaviour. The floor is a white plane with a soft screen-space pool of light around the pointer: warm close to it, drifting to a blue haze far away (the `haze*` values in each mood). Away from the centre the pool stretches into a beam across the screen along the sun's direction (`CONFIG.haze`). The crowd only runs to a word when the pointer is close to it (`CONFIG.gather`), keeps a circle clear around the word, and stands in front of it jumping with hands up; elsewhere excitement is local and more random. No figure is ever given a target inside a keep-out circle (`keepTargetOut`: a word, a placard, a film, a prop; a target that would be is moved to the edge), and everyone on the move steers round the circles in its path (`steerAroundKeepOuts`, the two overlapping words at the bottom taken as one), so nobody walks into a word's edge and presses against it. Three more rules keep them moving round the circles (`steerAroundCircle`, `steerAroundKeepOuts`): a target in the valley between two overlapping circles (allowed, since it is outside both) is reached through the mouth of the valley rather than by steering round the circle that encloses the pair; a figure against a circle whose target lies outward walks straight to it instead of sliding along the edge; and each figure remembers which way round it chose, so a target almost straight through a circle doesn't flip it every frame. Once a figure has arrived it stays settled until pushed more than a body length away (`a.settled`), so a nudge from a neighbour doesn't set it shuffling on the spot.

Nav: Work at the bottom (it takes you down), Contact top right (it takes you right), cold top left where the mouse arrives. The name is extruded on the plane in the middle of the home screen, built at runtime from the Inter outlines (opentype.js and the Inter woff both load from jsDelivr through the import map; vendor them with three.js before deploy); hovering it starts a party (`CONFIG.name`): some climb on and sit, some sit along the edge, a ring does the wave, a conga line snakes round, and three let off fireworks (`CONFIG.fireworks`). Away from home a small name link appears top left as the way back.

Films, captions and the name are all drawn with the same trapezoid perspective (`films.taper`, `films.squash`, `name.taper`); only the Work and Contact words float. The whole thumbnail is clickable.

## Run locally

Any static server works. From this folder:

    python3 -m http.server 5173

then open http://127.0.0.1:5173/. Browsers cache `style.css` and `main.js` between edits; a hard reload (Cmd-Shift-R) picks up changes. three.js loads from jsDelivr through the import map in `index.html` (pinned to 0.170.0), so the first load needs a connection.

Useful URL flags for tuning: `?debug` (fps, sim and draw cost, mood weights, and a `window.__stage` handle with `step(seconds)` to fast-forward the simulation), `?nobloom`, `?noshadows`, `?nofollow` (sun direction from the mood blend instead of the mouse), `?count=80`, `?touch` (phone behaviour on a desktop), `?still` (the reduced-motion frame).

## Where things live

- `main.js`: the whole scene. `CONFIG` at the top holds every colour, count, timing and flag.
- `site.js`: name, title, company and contact details. The placards carry the title's words, the About text and the plain film list say it, and the Contact screen shows the details and, if `ask` is set, a line above the form (empty values leave their line out; `ask` is empty now; Send is disabled until there is an email address).
- `films.js`: the film list and copy, in column order. Each film has a title, client, year, line, note and optionally a `status` word shown after the year (On air, Pitch, Unreleased, Cannes winner); `section` starts a new section of the column; `episodes` puts several films under one entry (Apple's four Ecosystems episodes), the thumbnail opening the first and the player offering the rest along the bottom. `public: false` keeps a film out of the build.
- `films/`: posters (`<id>.jpg`) and 720p web copies (`<id>.mp4`) cut by `tools/cut_films.py` from `../Films for portfolio`. Run it again after adding a film to that folder and to `FILMS` in the script.
- `sets.js`: prop arrangements for the Work column (benches, deckchairs, trees, lamps, a kiosk, blankets, parasols, bins), placed relative to each film. Every public film has its own themed set keyed by its id; `set: 'picnic'` in `films.js` overrides that, and films with neither cycle through the generic sets. Props are built from primitives in `props.js` (`PROP_KINDS`, about fifty kinds, nearly all made for one film: the hotel's velvet rope and luggage cart, Moretti's lawnmower and picnic table, the 99 ice cream, the peacock, the lighthouse and goal, the turbine, the beehives, the rocket and gantry, the three pigs' houses, Apple's desk, sofa and TV). One instanced mesh per kind; each carries a keep-out circle and seats the crowd takes when it rests. Only trees are shared between films.
- `style.css`: nav words, name, contact form, player, the About 'Start with' line.
- `tools/cloudflare/`: a range-request worker and routes file for a wrangler deploy to Cloudflare, not used on Vercel; see Deploy.
- `og.jpg`: the home-screen still for link previews (1800 x 945, the warm state), made in the browser with `window.__stage.snapshot()` under `?debug` and saved by hand.

## Phones

Below 700px (or portrait, `isNarrow()`) the site is framed for a phone: the view is `view.phoneZoom` times closer, so the figures read as people and the name fills about four fifths of the width (`name.phoneWidthFrac`), and the Work column uses its own arrangement: thumbnail nearly full width, caption underneath with a keep-out circle of its own so nobody walks over the words, props above the image only, and a keep-out the size of the poster. About is an HTML block in the contact form's style (`#about-text`, filled from `ABOUT_TEXT` and placed each frame like the form; the crowd rings it), with the 'Start with' line wrapped under it. Phones render at their own pixel ratio (capped at 2). The crowd is 60, the props halve, and bloom and shadows are off.

Touch devices have no hover, so the site runs itself more and answers scroll and taps: the pool of light follows a slow virtual pointer round the screen and jumps to a touch for a few seconds (`mood.cycleSeconds`, `mood.touchHold`); the party at the name plays once by itself soon after the placards are planted and again after any tap on the name (`name.welcomeAfter`, `name.tapParty`, long enough for the phrase to be spelled); in the Work column the film whose thumbnail is nearest the middle of the screen counts as hovered (`films.centreHover`), so the crowd wakes around it and the light tints to its poster as you scroll, and a tap on a thumbnail plays it. Navigation is the same as on a desktop: tap About or Contact to pan there, the name top left to come back, the words at the bottom to jump down the column. To try it on a phone on the same wifi, the `portfolio-phone` launch config serves the folder on every interface (port 5176); open this Mac's address at that port.

## Robustness

`fallback.js` builds a plain HTML list of the films (title, client, year, line, note, link) for screen readers and search; it is shown on its own if WebGL is missing or the scripts fail to start within nine seconds. Sections are history entries, so Back walks home. Posters load only when their film is within 1.6 screens, are cached across rebuilds, and captions are disposed on rebuild; resize is debounced. three.js is the min build from the CDN (vendoring is still the deploy step).

## Deploy

Vercel, by drag-and-drop at https://vercel.com/drop (signed in to a free Hobby account, so the project keeps its name): drag the upload folder, name the project `lucas-stanley`, Deploy; the site is at https://lucas-stanley.vercel.app/. This folder is a git repository pushed to the private GitHub repo Lucasarstanley/Portfolio-site; once the Vercel project is connected to it, every push to `main` goes live on its own (commit, push, done). `.vercelignore` keeps `README.md` and `tools/` out of each deployment. Dropping the folder onto the project in the Vercel dashboard still works as a manual deploy. The upload folder is a clean copy of this one without `README.md` and `tools/`, remade with

    rsync -a --delete --exclude README.md --exclude tools --exclude .DS_Store --exclude __pycache__ ./ "../OUTPUTS/Portfolio site/upload 2026-11-17/lucas-stanley-site/"

Why Vercel: Safari and iPhones only play a video from a server that answers a byte-range request with that range (a 206). Tested on 2026-11-17 with a two-byte range: Vercel and Netlify answer 206; Cloudflare's static hosting (Workers assets, which the dashboard's "Upload and deploy" creates, and Pages by two October 2026 reports) answers 200 with the whole file. Cloudflare's dashboard upload cannot run a worker to fix that (no node on this Mac, so no wrangler either), and Netlify's current free plan meters bandwidth in credits that come to about 15GB a month, too little for films. Vercel's Hobby plan is free, for personal non-commercial sites, with 100GB a month. `tools/cloudflare/` keeps the `_worker.js` (range → 206, tested in the browser on 21 cases) and `_routes.json` that a wrangler deploy to Cloudflare would need, should the site move there. `index.html` carries `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` while the site is only shared by link (delete the line to be found), and canonical, `og:url` and `og:image` point at https://lucas-stanley.vercel.app/ (change the three when the address differs). Still to do, before or after: `city` and `linkedin` in `site.js` are still empty, so those lines stay off the Contact screen (`email` is in, so Send works as a mailto link); vendor three.js, the addons and opentype, or keep the CDN (`fallback.js` covers a failure); bump the `?v=` query strings in `index.html` and the imports in `main.js` after every change (manual cache busting; easy to forget). The two long films are two-pass encodes at about 23MB each (`BITRATE` in `tools/cut_films.py`), well under Vercel Drop's 100MB file limit, so every web copy is self-hosted. `robots.txt` and `favicon.svg` are in place.

## Business

Small scripted bits the crowd does, all in `CONFIG.vignettes`: one figure always follows the cursor; in the cold state a group huddles like penguins and rotates; every half minute or so at home one of a queue to nowhere, a group photo (back row jumping, one latecomer) or a line of dominoes; in Work someone trips over a thumbnail edge and two others come and stand over him; the crowd near a film applauds after you have watched it; at the name party the conga picks up anyone it passes, and straight away the crowd sprints into HE'S PRETTY GOOD above the name (`vignettes.spell`): two lines where there is width, three on a narrow window, HE'S GOOD on a phone, the first layout whose letters come out at least `legible` figure heights tall; the formation fits itself to the figures free at that moment, borrowing the sitters, then shrinking, then spacing them out. Spellers are pinned to their point on arrival and ignore neighbours' pushing; a watchdog re-issues any lost task, lets latecomers straight through after `settle` seconds and places them outright after `place` more, so the phrase always completes. The stroke alphabet in `STROKE_LETTERS` needs a new letter drawn for any character it lacks.

Two cows can roam the plane everywhere, switched off for now (`cows.count` 0; 2 brings them back, and the cow version as it stood is saved in `OUTPUTS/Portfolio site/versions/2026-11-06 cows`). With them on (`CONFIG.cows`, model in `buildCowParts` in props.js, behaviour in `updateCows`): they amble, stop to graze with the head down, turn the head toward a nearby pointer, and every few seconds one paws the ground, head down, then charges the thickest knot of people and chases whoever is nearest, swapping victims as they get away. Anyone within `fearRadius` of a cow (twice that in front of a charge) who is not on a bit of scripted business drops what they are doing, sitters included, and sprints straight away from it with arms in the air for `fearHold` seconds, then goes back to whatever they were doing. Anyone a moving cow runs into (`knockRadius`) is bowled over: flung along the cow's line, harder the faster it was going, flat for `downFor` seconds, then up and running. The cows keep a wider berth of every keep-out than a person does, stay out of the name and the spelling, and walk back in from an edge after a scroll or a page change.

## Work column

The column is seven client films in the order of `films.js` (Airbnb, then the Andrex pitch, then Nectar, Gren, Ninety-Nine and the two Heineken pitches), each with its status word after the year where there is one; then a Short films section (a wider gap and a floor heading, `section` in `films.js`) with the two personal films; then an Archive section with the rest, Apple's four episodes as one entry. Idle it is warm (`mood.workIdle`) and the pool of light follows the mouse as it does at home (`films.lightPull` 0; raise it to lean the pool toward the thumbnails); hovering a film wakes the crowd around it and pulls the haze and sun toward that poster's dominant colour (`films.tint`, sampled from the poster at load in `posterColour`).

## Home screen extras

On arrival figures run in from the left carrying placards, one word of the title each (CREATIVE, LEAD from `site.js`; `CONFIG.placards` for the sizes) and plant them in a row under the name, where they stay as the title. Arriving on another section plants them straight away. The bottom nav has Work and Short films side by side; Short films scrolls to that section of the column. Top left is one slot: About at home, the name (the way back) everywhere else. About pans one screen to the left (`world.aboutLeft`), where a short text lies on the floor (`ABOUT_TEXT` in main.js, in Lucas's voice, the title read from `site.js`) with a 'Start with Nectar, Airbnb or Gren' line under it in the nav words' style (an HTML line placed each frame like the contact form; each name opens that film) and the whole crowd rings it and jumps; each figure steers round the text's keep-out circle to a spot of its own and keeps it (`steerAroundCircle`, `gatherAt.full`). On the home screen a slight white shimmer sweeps over the three nav words in turn (`style.css`, `shimmer`).

## Home moods

The crowd rests in the cold state until the pointer is on one of three things: the Work word, the Contact word, or the name (its letters' box, with a little hysteresis so the gaps between letters don't flicker). The sun and the pool of light still follow the mouse everywhere; figures inside the pool perk up a little (`CONFIG.torch`), and Work and Contact brighten as the light passes over them. The huddle is on a timer (`vignettes.huddle`) so it is not the permanent resting state. On touch devices, where there is no hover, the home screen stays in the cold state for now.

## Contact form

The form shows the ask from `site.js` above the fields and the city, email and LinkedIn under the button, and builds a mailto link to `SITE.email` on submit (Send is disabled while that is empty). On Netlify it can become a real form by adding `data-netlify="true"` and removing the submit handler.
